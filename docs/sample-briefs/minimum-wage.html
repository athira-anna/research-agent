Topic as understood: This research brief examines how adjustments to mandated wage floors impact labor market outcomes such as hiring, hours, and total employment.

Assumptions: The analysis applies to any geographic region and historical time period unless specified otherwise. Minimum wage refers strictly to legally mandated federal, state, or municipal hourly wage floors.

1. Summary

  • While the Congressional Budget Office projects that raising the federal minimum wage to $15 an hour by 2025 would put 1.3 million Americans out of work and lift 1.3 million out of poverty while increasing pay for 17 million people, the Economic Policy Institute reports that 90% of studies find no or only small disemployment effects with a median own-wage elasticity of employment of -0.04.
  • NPR and Reason note that the current federal minimum wage is $7.25, with NPR stating that 29 states, the District of Columbia, the Virgin Islands, and Guam have set higher standards, while Reason notes that 10 others adopted increases through specific legislation or ballot initiatives.
  • The Center on Wisconsin Strategy reports that recent federal minimum wage increases raised earnings for low-wage workers in Wisconsin without causing job loss.
  • Significant gaps remain regarding which demographic groups experience the highest rates of job loss and how changes in the minimum wage impact average weekly hours worked compared to total headcount.
